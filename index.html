MSc Thesis

HGV Driver Simulator for Roll Stability

Cranfield University · 2026 · Individual research project

Real-time driver-in-the-loop simulator that estimates how close a 28.8-tonne 8×8 test truck is to wheel lift while it is being driven.

  • Built a four-axle, two-axle-steered lateral model coupled to single-DoF roll and longitudinal dynamics in MATLAB/Simulink
  • Verified within 0.2% of the analytical solution; yaw rate validated against test-truck data (NRMSE 14.8–16.5%)

Result: The predicted wheel-lift threshold roughly halves, from ~0.4 g to ~0.2 g, at the 0.8–0.9 Hz roll resonance. CoG height and longitudinal position are the dominant sensitivities.

  • Simulink
  • Unreal Engine

MSc Module

Double Wishbone Arm Optimisation

Cranfield University · 2025 · Individual assignment

Material selection, topology optimisation and a fatigue check for the upper and lower arms of a double wishbone suspension.

  • Chose 6061-T6 aluminium from Ashby charts in Granta, then ranked the shortlist with SAW and TOPSIS
  • Calculated corner loads by hand (3 g vertical, 1 g longitudinal and lateral) and optimised both arms in OptiStruct against a 2 mm displacement limit

Result: Arms about 34% lighter (6.887 to 4.556 kg), with 1.95 mm peak displacement inside the 2 mm limit.

MEng Industry Project

LOFT-E Loft Leg Thermal Analysis

Lancaster University with Cumbria Loft Boarding · 2021 · Group of five

How much heat a steel loft-boarding leg lets through the insulation, and what it would take to meet building regulations.

  • Built an ANSYS steady-state thermal model of a section of loft, refining the mesh over 14 steps from 0.59 to 0.86 average element quality
  • Compared leg materials by U-value and fitted a curve to find the thermal conductivity limit

Result: No metal leg meets the 0.16 W/m²K ceiling target; the material would need 0.92 W/m·K or less. The company publishes our report on its product page.

F1 25 Aero Map Regression

Python · October 2026 · Code public on GitHub

Separating front and rear ride-height effects on axle downforce, from my own controlled test runs in F1 25.

  • Fitted a two-variable model in SciPy that matched Excel's LINEST exactly
  • Tested it against a quadratic model with leave-one-out cross-validation, with pytest checks on the code

Personal Project

Quadricycle Brake Balance Model

Python and FreeCAD · September 2026 – present

The first design question in my own two-seat quadricycle vehicle model: where should the brake balance sit?

  • Built a Python braking model with load transfer and brake balance; a brake-split sweep agreed with theory within 0.02 percentage points
  • Tested the effect of payload with the split fixed at 65% front

Result: Ideal front share of 55–71% across 0.2–0.8 grip. With a fixed split, two occupants and luggage cut usable grip from 92% to 83% and lengthened the stop from 90 km/h from 86.7 m to 96.0 m.
