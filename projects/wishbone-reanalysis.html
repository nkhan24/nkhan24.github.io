Double Wishbone Re-analysis

Re-checking my own Cranfield suspension arm study in open-source tools, because I don't trust its fatigue result.

Why I am doing this

My Cranfield wishbone study passed its fatigue target, but the loads came from a smooth drive cycle, so I don't trust the result. I am rebuilding the study in free, open-source tools to test the topology optimisation again and re-check the statics and fatigue behind it.

What I have done so far

  • Geometry and mesh: rebuilt both arms in FreeCAD and meshed them in PrePoMax: 122,809 quadratic tetrahedral elements and 235,470 nodes, with aluminium at E = 70 GPa and ν = 0.33. The baseline mass matched the Cranfield model within 0.1%.
  • Learning the method first: ran the BESO (bi-directional evolutionary structural optimisation) scripts with the CalculiX solver on a single small steel tab before touching the arms, to see how the optimiser behaves and when it stops.
  • Protected rings: kept 8 mm solid rings at the seven bores.
  • Stiffness constraint: added the 2 mm knuckle displacement limit from the original study. When a step breaks the limit, the optimiser adds material back.
  • More load cases: moved from two load cases to four (±1 g longitudinal, ±1 g lateral and 3 g vertical at the knuckle), matching the hand-calculated cases from the original study.
  • Solver failure: one long run stopped at iteration 63 when CalculiX could not open its input file. I re-ran that step and restarted from the state saved at iteration 60, rather than starting again.

Next steps

  • Finish the four-load-case run and check it has converged.
  • Model the optimised arm as real geometry and put it back into the suspension model in place of the original.
  • Re-check the statics and fatigue against the original targets, and challenge the result rather than accept it.

About the numbers on this page

The optimisation is still running, so this page shows the method and the checks, not a final mass. A raw topology result is not a manufacturable arm, so I won't compare its mass with the 34% from the original study until the rebuilt arm has been re-checked.

Tools: FreeCAD, PrePoMax, CalculiX, BESO (Python)
